01 — Orientation

Getting Started

You wake with no memory of who you were. Whoever that was doesn't matter here — what matters is who you become.

You'll arrive at a quiet grove with no roads pointing anywhere in particular. That's deliberate. Everything past the tree line, towns, powers, dangers — is found, not signposted.

Money

/bal to check your balance, /pay <player> <amount> to send coin to someone else.

Selling what you find

There's no universal shop for offloading loot, instead, certain merchants buy categories of goods. Stand near one, hold the item, and type:

Command /sell — the nearest qualifying merchant quotes you a price per item and for your whole stack, then you reply 1 to sell it all, 2 to sell just one, or cancel.

Blacksmiths buy ore, ingots, raw metal, and gems. Alchemists buy brewing ingredients. Nobody buys everything, figuring out who wants what is part of finding your feet.

02 — Character

Choose Your Origin

Every origin carries real abilities — some convenient, some strange, a couple of them dangerous to misuse.

Check your current origin and pick a new one with /origin. The full list spans grounded and strange alike; flyers, swimmers, climbers, creatures of fire and night, and many more besides. Each comes with its own strengths, its own limitations, and its own way of seeing the world.

03 — Lore

Covenant of the Sword

Purity of blood, purity of purpose. The Light asks three things of its faithful — obedience, tithes, and mostly tithes.

The Covenant is a theocracy built on the belief that human and elf blood carry a purity worth defending with steel, if the Light requires it. Everyone else is, at best, tolerated. Its border towns are garrisoned and devout in equal measure; its capital keeps a cathedral at its heart and a great deal more self-regard than the frontier can afford.

Generations ago, the Covenant marched south to bring its Light to the desert at swordpoint. It did not go the way its priests still like to remember. The desert, as it turned out, does not kneel easily and the war left a border neither side has fully stopped fighting since, even now that the clashing of swords have quieted to trade routes and old grudges.

Beneath the sermons runs a real fear: that the old magic never left, that it only went quiet, and that somewhere among the faithful it is stirring again. The Covenant burns what it cannot explain. It has burned people for less than a rumor.

A church stands at the center of it all, for those willing to take an oath and live — or die — by what it costs them.

04 — Lore

The Empire

No kings taxing your sweat, no priests judging your blood. Only the market and whoever was paid to enforce it.

The Empire is anarcho-capitalist to its core: a desert republic held together by contracts, mercenaries, and the shared understanding that everything has a price. There is an Emperor, technically, sitting somewhere at the top of it — but his word carries exactly as far as his coin does, no further. Out here, contracts rule. Break one, and it isn't a judge who comes for you.

Freedom is the Empire's founding claim, and it means exactly what it says: the freedom to buy, to sell, to rise, to fall, to be sold yourself if your luck or your debts run out. Slavery is not blessed by the state. It is simply never stopped by it.

The Empire remembers its founding as a war for the right to worship or refuse to worship as it pleased, fought against a northern power that demanded purity and offered only the sword to those who wouldn't kneel to it. Whatever the truth of that history, the Empire has never forgotten who marched on it first.

It has little patience for priests and less for their god. What it has no shortage of is buyers for steel, for secrets, and for whatever else a desperate soul might bring to market.

05 — The Weave

Magic

The Covenant calls it heresy. The Empire calls it a product. Neither of them can stop it.

Magic exists, and almost no one you meet in the open will admit to knowing anything real about it. Ask in the wrong town and you'll be called a heretic to your face. Ask in the right one and someone may quietly point you toward a higher price than you were expecting....including your soul

The truth of it — where the weave can actually be learned, and from whom — isn't written here. It has to be discovered, the same way everyone who knows it now once found it themselves: by leaving the roads, and paying attention to what the world quietly implies.

06 — Survival

Danger & Death

Dying here is rarely simple, and running from a fight is never free.

Combat tagging

Land or take a hit rom a player or a hostile mob? and you're tagged for 15 seconds! Elytra and Mending are disabled while tagged.

Do not log out mid-fight Disconnecting while tagged kills you outright. This applies to PvP and to fights with mobs and hostile NPCs alike there is no safe logout from a fight you started.

Going down

Most deaths don't end things immediately. You'll go downed instead — barely alive, slowed, with a 30-second bleedout clock. Every command is locked out until you're back up.

Controls, downed & reviving Sneak near a downed ally to start reviving them — hold it, stay close, and it takes a few seconds to bring them back up.
Right-click a downed player to loot what they're carrying.
Shift + left-click a downed player to hoist them onto your back and carry them somewhere safer; shift again to set them back down.
If you're the one downed and the fight's unwinnable, hold sneak on yourself for a few seconds to surrender outright instead of waiting to bleed out.

The world gets harder the further you wander

Mob difficulty scales with distance from civilization. Close to the towns, the wilds are forgiving. Far enough out, they stop being forgiving entirely and something considerably worse than a zombie may be patrolling out there, waiting.

07 — The Other Side

The Nether: Soul Hearts

Every soul that crosses over leaves something behind.

Step through a portal and a chest appears beside you, holding a Heart of <your name> — glowing, and precisely as vulnerable as it sounds.

  • Leave the Nether safely, and your heart travels back into your inventory with you. Nothing lost.
  • If the chest is broken — or the heart itself is destroyed while you're still in the Nether — you become banished. Every attempt to leave drags you straight back to where it happened.
  • Destroying an already-collected heart, safely back home, costs you nothing but the item.
If a friend is banished Someone out there keeps a ledger of every banished soul on the server, and can be paid to release one. Whether you ever find her before or after you need her is up to you.
